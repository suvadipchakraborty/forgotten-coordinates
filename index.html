About Forgotten Coordinates

A small atlas of places people left behind, matched to the cities people live in today.

Purpose

Most of us know our own city well and know almost nothing about the abandoned towns, lost colonies and ruined sites sitting a few hours away. Forgotten Coordinates pairs the two. Name a city and you get its ghost twin: the nearest abandoned settlement or archaeological ruin, with exact coordinates, distance, direction and a written history.

How it works

  1. Your city name is turned into coordinates.
  2. The Wikidata archive is searched for abandoned settlements and archaeological sites within 500 km, widening to 2,000 km if nothing is found.
  3. The nearest site with a written record is picked and its history is fetched from Wikipedia.
  4. The distance and bearing are calculated along the great circle (the shortest path over the globe) and drawn on the map.

What counts as a ghost twin

Ghost towns, abandoned villages and archaeological sites that have a Wikipedia article. Places without a documented history are skipped, so the nearest twin on the map is not always the nearest ruin in reality.

Data and credits

Place search by Nominatim, sites from Wikidata, text and photos from Wikipedia (their licences apply), map tiles and data by OpenStreetMap contributors. The app has no accounts, no tracking and stores only your recent searches in your own browser to load them faster.

Good to know

The dossier is the opening of the Wikipedia article, so quality varies by site. Search results come from public services and can be slow at busy times. Repeat searches load instantly.
